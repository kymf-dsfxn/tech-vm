# Strunk's Clarity Rules

These are the load-bearing composition rules from William Strunk Jr.'s *The Elements of Style* (1918, public domain). They add force and craft to the STE form. STE tells you how to be unambiguous. Strunk tells you how to be strong.

Where STE and Strunk conflict, resolve by mode (see the precedence table in `SKILL.md`). In standard mode, these craft rules win.

## Rule 8. One paragraph per topic

Make the paragraph the unit of composition. Each topic gets its own paragraph. A new paragraph signals to the reader that a new step in the thought has started.

## Rule 9. Begin each paragraph with a topic sentence

State the point at or near the start. Let the following sentences develop it. End the paragraph in line with the beginning, not with a digression.

## Rule 10. Use the active voice

The active voice is more direct and vigorous than the passive.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| My first visit to Boston will always be remembered by me. | I shall always remember my first visit to Boston. |
| There were a great number of dead leaves lying on the ground. | Dead leaves covered the ground. |
| A survey of this region was made in 1900. | This region was surveyed in 1900. |

Strunk allows the passive when the object is the true subject of the sentence. "The dramatists of the Restoration are little esteemed today" is right in a paragraph about those dramatists. Note that STE strict mode bans the passive in procedures regardless.

## Rule 11. Put statements in positive form

Make definite assertions. Use "not" for denial or contrast, never as evasion.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| He was not very often on time. | He usually came late. |
| did not remember | forgot |
| did not pay any attention to | ignored |
| not important | trifling |

The reader wants to be told what is, not only what is not.

## Rule 12. Use definite, specific, concrete language

Prefer the specific to the general, the concrete to the abstract.

| Vague | Concrete |
| --- | --- |
| A period of unfavorable weather set in. | It rained every day for a week. |
| He showed satisfaction as he took possession of his well-earned reward. | He grinned as he pocketed the coin. |
| An issue occurred during processing. | The parser rejected the malformed header. |

Concrete words call up a picture. Abstract words make the reader do the work.

## Rule 13. Omit needless words

Vigorous writing is concise. Make every word tell. This is the rule that does the most work.

| Wordy | Tight |
| --- | --- |
| the question as to whether | whether |
| there is no doubt but that | no doubt |
| used for fuel purposes | used for fuel |
| he is a man who | he |
| in a hasty manner | hastily |
| owing to the fact that | because |
| call your attention to the fact that | remind you |
| His brother, who is a member of the same firm | His brother, a member of the same firm |

Revise "the fact that" out of every sentence. Cut "who is" and "which was" when they add nothing.

## Rule 14. Avoid a succession of loose sentences

A string of two-clause sentences joined by "and", "but", or "so" becomes monotonous. Vary the structure. This is why standard mode does not impose a hard length cap: rhythm matters, and every sentence the same length reads as sing-song.

## Rule 15. Express coordinate ideas in similar form

Use parallel construction for parallel ideas. Likeness of form shows likeness of content.

| Not parallel | Parallel |
| --- | --- |
| Formerly, science was taught by the textbook method, while now the laboratory method is employed. | Formerly, science was taught by the textbook method. Now it is taught by the laboratory method. |
| A time not for words, but action. | A time not for words, but for action. |

## Rule 16. Keep related words together

Word position shows relationship. Keep the subject near its verb, the modifier near what it modifies, and the relative pronoun near its antecedent.

| Scattered | Together |
| --- | --- |
| He only found two mistakes. | He found only two mistakes. |
| All the members were not present. | Not all the members were present. |
| Wordsworth, in the fifth book of The Excursion, gives a minute description of this church. | In the fifth book of The Excursion, Wordsworth gives a minute description of this church. |

## Rule 18. Place the emphatic words at the end

The end of the sentence is the position of prominence. Put the new or most important idea there.

| Flat | Emphatic |
| --- | --- |
| This steel is principally used for making razors, because of its hardness. | Because of its hardness, this steel is used mainly for making razors. |
| Humanity has hardly advanced in fortitude since that time, though it has advanced in many other ways. | Humanity, since that time, has advanced in many ways, but hardly in fortitude. |

The same principle applies to the sentences of a paragraph and the paragraphs of a document.
